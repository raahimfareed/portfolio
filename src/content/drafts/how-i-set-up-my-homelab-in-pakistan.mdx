### Cabling

I needed the following cables to get it all connected and working.
- **1x DP Cable**: so that I can easily install the operating system (Optiplex 9010 only has VGA and DP, I chose to work with DP)

  This is only needed until the OS is installed, after that it's kinda useless, unless you run into any problems and have to connect the server to a monitor again :). Prices vary from place to place, just get the cheapest one to get the job done, unless you already have one.

- **1x Cat 6a 10M Ethernet Cable**: To connect my router to my switch. Get at least a cat 6 cable, don't go below that. I chose this size so that it can comfortably reach my room. Again, prices vary from place to place, measure the amount you need and get a good quality one because your whole setup will be running on this cable.

- **4x Cat 6a 1-2M Ethernet Cables**: Originally I needed only 2, from switch to PC and Homelab, later on I expanded my setup so I needed some extra cables to connect everything.

- **1x Extension Lead with a lot of sockets**: So that I could plugin my PC, homelab and monitor among other things.

### Digital Signage PC: Custom Router

**Price:** Rs.20,000  
**Model:** Shuttle DS-81  
**From:** Soan Gardens, Islamabad

This one is a bit interesting. I've had a lot of hardware components in my wishlist. *A custom router* was one of them. So, generally, we're given a router by our ISP (In my case, Nayatel gave us a Huawei ONT/Router). For majority of people, that's enough. My father never changed the router in the past. Sure he got WiFi extenders, but the actual router given to us by the ISP was still there. To have more fine grained control and power though, a custom solution is required.

To cut it short, a custom router is a router you make from your own hardware (could be a smol computer, or a big computer, I'm not judging). The basic job is still to communicate with the ISP and ultimately the internet, but it comes with its own perks, like your own firewall, ad blocker, plugins like VPN (Not the kinds that we use to escape regional blocks, but the kind that allows you to connect to your server remotely while you're away from home).

With that being said, Shuttle DS81 is originally made for digital signage I think. I found it off of Facebook Marketplace near Soan Garden, Islamabad. ## Getting Everything Connected: The Actual Network

I'm by profession a Software Engineer, and by hobby a gamer. I had 0 interest in computer networks until I actually setup my own server. Now, you don't have to know everything, I certainly don't, but it helps knowing some basics (which I won't teach because.).

### Operating System
To actually start working, I first installed Ubuntu 24 LTS. It's a distribution of linux. This is preferred over windows for servers because it has high security, it's very stable, had good docker support (we use this a lot in self hosting) and it's free (open source baby!).

### Unmanaged Switch

In a previous section, I mentioned how I bought a TP-Link unmanaged switch. Think of it as an ethernet port extender. It's just that in simple words. Have less ports? Just add an unmanaged switch. There's more to it of course, but for our use case, this is exactly what it is.

How does it work though? Simple, It has n amount of eth ports, in our case 5. Plug one end of the eth cable into your main router, and the other end in ANY eth port of the unmanaged switch, it doesn't matter. For cleanliness and order I plugged mine into the left most port.

Once that is done, I connected both my PC and my homelab server into free ports in the switch and finally connected the switch adapter into my extension board.

That's about it.
